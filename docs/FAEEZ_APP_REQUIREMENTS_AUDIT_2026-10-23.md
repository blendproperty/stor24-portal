## Exactly what Faeez needs to supply

1. **Working checklists:** current Word files for daily, weekly and monthly work. For each activity: responsible role/person, covering person, due day/time, required evidence or counts, who signs off, and what happens when a snag is found. Include walkthrough, locked-unit checks, padlock stock, payment checking, debtor calls and monthly overlocking if used.
2. **CSO report examples:** a blank template and one redacted completed example of each check-in/check-out report. Explain whether it means attendance, shift handover or operational checks. Identify mandatory fields, attachments, manager visibility, sign-off and retention needs.
3. **SiteLink examples:** screenshots or a short screen recording of the bulk adjustments and back-office settings he wants retained. For each, show the starting selection, action, preview/approval and expected result. Redact customer data.
4. **Staff/access matrix:** names, job roles and stores; which managers need which reports/actions; which CSOs are authorised and trained; the person who approves each grant; and who covers absences. No passwords are needed.
5. **Lockout policy:** overdue eligibility and cutoff/grace dates, disputed-payment and other exceptions, required notices, approver, staff responsible for gate restriction versus physical overlock, evidence required, and the approval/evidence needed to restore access. The 7th is an example until confirmed for the store.
6. **Outlook decision:** one-way Outlook display, app-to-Outlook creation or two-way sync; personal/shared calendars and exact work-account/calendar names; meeting ownership; handling of edits, cancellations, duplicates and sync failures. Account consent will be needed later through the real Microsoft flow, not by sharing credentials.
7. **Acceptance examples:** walk through one normal manager day and one month-end/lockout cycle after the above are mapped. Identify who will accept the workflow and which actual tablet will be used for the separate onboarding test.

Brett/commercial owner decision, separate from Faeez's documents: this branch adjusts standard inventory list rates only. Any future change to existing customer rental charges requires the existing governed rental/billing workflow and explicit agreed commercial rules.

## Local review and validation

- Use an isolated development database; generate Prisma and apply migrations before running the app. New additive migration: `20261023120000_scheduled_work` adds nullable task start/end/unit fields and a unit index/foreign key. Do not point tests/bootstrap at production.
- Run `npm ci`, `npm run db:generate`, `npm run db:migrate:deploy`, then `npm run dev` with the project's normal local authentication/configuration. A genuinely empty isolated installation may require the existing guarded bootstrap procedure; do not use it against a populated database.
- Open **Units & rates → Adjust selected unit prices**. Choose size/range, tick intended units, preview and inspect every current/proposed rate. Saving a real rate is a real mutation; use synthetic local units for review.
- Open **Calendar → New scheduled work**. Choose store/owner, start/end in SAST and a finite recurrence. Review count and final occurrence. Edit/complete an individual occurrence; verify siblings and completed history. Existing customer appointments retain their source workflows.
- Recurrence is intentionally finite and explicit: at most 366 daily or 24 monthly occurrences. No background service replenishes a series. Closed records cannot be reopened through the generic Operations endpoint.
- Run `npx tsx --test tests/bulk-unit-pricing.test.ts tests/scheduled-work-policy.test.ts`, the three new `tests/integration/*` files on guarded localhost PostgreSQL, and `node scripts/test-faeez-workflows.mjs`. The protected workflows include those new checks. Browser fixtures use real components, compiled styles and the bundled font; synthetic data is not staff acceptance.
- Validation evidence is recorded in the dated canonical `PROJECT_CONTEXT.md` entry and local `output/` logs/screenshots. Initial compatibility failures were test-fixture omissions exposed by the new service imports/delegates; they were repaired while retaining the original boundary and rollback assertions. The first visual pass exposed form/layout defects, which were corrected and rerun.
